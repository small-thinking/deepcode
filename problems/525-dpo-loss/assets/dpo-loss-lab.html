Why beta is not simply “a bigger update”

At a fixed margin, ∂ℓ/∂gπ = −β sigmoid(−βΔ). At Δ = 0 its magnitude is β/2. When Δ is already positive, a larger β can saturate sigmoid and make the gradient smaller.

In the underlying reward-minus-KL objective, β is the coefficient of the KL penalty to the reference. For a fixed reward function, larger β favors staying closer to reference: π*(y|x) ∝ πref(y|x) exp(r(y,x)/β). This is an optimum-level interpretation, not a guarantee about every finite training run.

For a target preference likelihood q, the required margin is Δ = log(q/(1−q)) / β. A larger β therefore needs less policy/reference separation to represent the same preference confidence.

From one pair to a batch

The training data label chosen as preferred. Independent pair likelihoods multiply; their negative log becomes a sum. The exercise returns the mean across B pairs. Each row's policy log-probability gradients are −β sigmoid(−βΔ)/B for chosen and the opposite for rejected.
